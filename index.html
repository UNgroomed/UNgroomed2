UNgroomed is an Oklahoma-based nonprofit being built to support girls and women affected by grooming, abuse, exploitation, coercion, manipulation, and related trauma.

What is grooming?

Grooming is a pattern of behavior used to gain access, build trust, test boundaries, create secrecy or dependence, and make exploitation easier. It can happen in person or online.

What is coercion?

Coercion means using pressure, threats, manipulation, intimidation, deception, or abuse of power to influence another person's choices.

What is exploitation?

Exploitation occurs when someone takes unfair or harmful advantage of another person for power, profit, sexual access, labor, control, or another benefit.

A fact worth remembering

Reported numbers do not capture every incident. Sexual abuse is widely underreported, so official crime data should not be mistaken for the total amount of harm occurring.

Where Do I Go From Here?

You do not have to rebuild your whole life today. You only need one next step. This is your place to breathe, reset, learn, care for yourself, and begin creating a life that feels like yours again.

Your next chapter can be small. Take a shower. Brush your hair. Drink some water. Step outside. Make the appointment. Apply for the class. Pray. Rest. Write one goal down. Tiny steps still count.

🌱 Start With You

✨ Beauty & Self-Care

You deserve to feel cared for—not because you have to look a certain way, but because your body and your sense of self deserve kindness. Try a simple reset: shower, moisturize, brush and style your hair, clean your nails, put on comfortable clothes you love, and give yourself a little time in the mirror without criticizing what you see.

  • Create a simple morning and nighttime routine.
  • Keep a small “feel-good” beauty kit ready.
  • Try a new hairstyle, lip color, skincare routine, or outfit just for fun.
  • Care for your hair, skin, teeth, sleep, hydration, and movement.
  • Remember: self-care is care—not perfection.

💗 Confidence & Self-Worth

What happened to you does not have to become the definition of you. Practice speaking to yourself the way you would speak to someone you love.

  • Write down three things you survived, learned, or accomplished.
  • Replace “I should be farther along” with “I am moving forward.”
  • Set one boundary this week.
  • Celebrate progress that nobody else can see.
  • Do one thing each day that reminds you who you are outside of what happened.

🧼 Reset Your Space

Your surroundings can affect how overwhelmed you feel. You do not need a perfect home—just one calmer corner to begin.

  • Pick up trash first.
  • Choose one surface to clear.
  • Make your bed or refresh your sleeping area.
  • Open a window or change the lighting.
  • Put on music and work for just ten minutes.

🧠 Everyday Self-Help

When everything feels like too much, shrink the goal. Focus on the next safe, healthy action instead of solving your entire life at once.

  • Eat something nourishing and drink water.
  • Take a slow breath and notice five things around you.
  • Write down what is bothering you instead of carrying every thought in your head.
  • Choose one task for today and one for tomorrow.
  • Reach out to someone safe when you need connection.

🕊️ Spiritual Growth

Explore prayer, reflection, meditation, scripture, faith communities, gratitude, nature, or other practices that help you reconnect with meaning and hope. You get to explore spirituality at your own pace.

🧘 Peace & Grounding

Build a calmer daily rhythm with grounding exercises, healthy coping skills, trauma-informed support, counseling, sleep routines, movement, and moments of quiet.

💄 Little Beauty Resets

Hair

Detangle gently, condition, protect your ends, refresh your style, or try something completely different. A simple hairstyle can be a small way to feel put together when everything else feels messy.

Skin

Keep it simple: gentle cleansing, moisturizer, and sun protection are a good foundation. Avoid harsh DIY treatments or anything that irritates your skin.

Makeup

Makeup can be playful self-expression. Try one feature at a time—a fresh brow, soft lip, glowing skin, or a dramatic eye—without making your appearance a measure of your worth.

Style

Wear something that makes you feel comfortable, powerful, pretty, relaxed, or simply like yourself. Your style belongs to you.

🧭 When You Don't Know What To Do

Try this: Ask yourself, “What would make tomorrow 5% easier?”

  1. Choose one thing that helps your body.
  2. Choose one thing that helps your mind.
  3. Choose one thing that helps your future.
  4. Choose one person or safe resource you can connect with.
  5. Stop for the day when you have done enough.

You are allowed to rebuild slowly. You are allowed to change direction. You are allowed to have good days and bad days. Progress does not have to look impressive to be real.

Keep going. You are not required to have everything figured out before you begin. Take care of the person you are today while making room for the person you are becoming. Come back whenever you need another next step.

Grooming 101

Grooming is a pattern of behaviors used to gain access, build trust, create secrecy or dependence, test boundaries, normalize inappropriate behavior, and increase control or exploitation. It can happen in person or online.

The Grooming Pattern

  1. Access and opportunity
  2. Attention or special treatment
  3. Trust and emotional connection
  4. Testing boundaries
  5. Secrecy and isolation
  6. Normalization or sexualization
  7. Control, exploitation or threats

Not every case follows every step or happens in this order. The pattern and context matter.

Why Grooming Can Work

Manipulation can change what feels normal gradually. Attachment, fear, admiration, dependence, shame, threats, isolation and hope can coexist. A person can care about or defend someone who is harming them.

Coercion & Control

Coercion can include pressure, threats, intimidation, blackmail, isolation, economic control, abuse of authority, or exploiting fear and dependence. A person may technically say “yes” while being subjected to coercive circumstances; legal consent questions are fact-specific.

Online Grooming

Watch for rapid intimacy, secrecy, moving conversations off-platform, sexualized conversation, requests for images, gifts or money, fake identities, isolation from trusted people, threats, or attempts to meet secretly.

Sextortion

Sextortion generally involves threatening to expose intimate images or information unless the target complies with demands. Preserve evidence when safe, avoid negotiating alone, and seek qualified law-enforcement or victim-support help.

Child Sexual Abuse

Children cannot be treated as responsible for an adult's abusive conduct. Oklahoma has mandatory reporting requirements when a person has reason to believe a child is a victim of abuse or neglect. Follow current Oklahoma law and agency procedures.

Sexual Exploitation

Exploitation can involve using a person's vulnerability, trust, images, labor, sexuality, dependence or circumstances for another person's benefit. The exact legal meaning depends on the statute and facts.

Human Trafficking

Trafficking is a specific legal concept, not simply another word for abuse. Oklahoma law addresses trafficking and child trafficking. A situation should be assessed using the current statutory definitions.

Boundaries

Healthy boundaries include privacy, bodily autonomy, age-appropriate relationships, freedom to say no, freedom to seek help, and relationships that do not depend on secrecy or fear.

Consent & Capacity

Consent is not simply the absence of a “no.” Age, capacity, coercion, force, threats, unconsciousness and other circumstances can change the legal analysis. UNgroomed provides education, not legal determinations.

Responding to a Disclosure

  1. Stay calm.
  2. Listen.
  3. Thank them for telling you.
  4. Avoid blame and interrogation.
  5. Do not promise absolute secrecy.
  6. If a child is involved, follow mandatory-reporting law.
  7. Connect them with qualified support.

Things NOT to Say

Avoid “Why didn't you leave?”, “Why did you trust them?”, “Why didn't you tell me?”, or “Are you sure?” Try: “I’m glad you told me,” “You deserve help,” and “What would help you feel safer right now?”

Why Didn't I Leave?

There may be fear, attachment, threats, isolation, dependence, shame, children involved, lack of housing, trauma responses, or concern about what will happen next. Leaving is not a simple test of whether abuse occurred.

Survivor Myths

Myth: Grooming always looks romantic. Reality: It may look like friendship, mentorship, protection, gifts or authority.

Myth: Returning means wanting abuse. Reality: Attachment and coercion can complicate leaving.

Myth: Only strangers groom. Reality: Grooming can involve known adults, peers, authority figures or online contacts.

Healing

Healing is not a deadline or a single treatment. It can include safety, counseling, medical care, education, work, supportive relationships, boundaries, creativity, spirituality, community, or simply getting through today. The survivor chooses the path whenever possible.

Digital Safety

Use strong unique passwords and multi-factor authentication. Review account access and location sharing. Be cautious if a device may be monitored. Preserve threatening messages when safe. Seek expert digital-safety help when necessary.

For Parents & Caregivers

Focus on connection rather than interrogation. Teach body autonomy and online safety. Watch for unexplained secrecy, sudden adult attention, unusual gifts, fear around a person or device, sexualized behavior that is developmentally concerning, or attempts to isolate the child. One sign alone does not prove abuse.

For Teens

You do not owe someone sexual images, secrecy, access to your accounts, or a meeting. A person who says “prove you love me” by crossing a boundary is asking for control, not proof of love.

For Professionals & Volunteers

Maintain boundaries. Do not investigate allegations. Document objectively. Protect privacy. Know your reporting duties. Refer outside your competence. Never create secret relationships with a minor.

How to Help a Friend

Listen, believe the need for support, ask what they want, offer choices, help locate qualified services, and respect adult autonomy. Do not confront an alleged offender yourself or make promises you cannot keep.

Oklahoma Law

This is educational information, not legal advice. Verify current Oklahoma Statutes before relying on any legal information.

Mandatory reporting

Oklahoma Title 10A provides that every person having reason to believe a child under 18 is a victim of abuse or neglect shall report the matter immediately to the Department of Human Services. The statute provides the reporting framework and specific rules for school employees. Read Oklahoma Title 10A.

Child abuse

Oklahoma criminal law separately addresses child abuse. The precise offense and punishment depend on the current statute and facts. UNgroomed should link to the current statute rather than reduce the law to a single sentence.

Sexual offenses

Oklahoma law contains multiple sexual offenses and classifications. Age, force, incapacity, relationship, conduct and other facts can change the applicable offense and penalty.

Grooming

“Grooming” is useful as an educational description of a pattern of manipulation, but not every grooming behavior is a standalone criminal offense. Specific conduct can be criminal under other Oklahoma statutes. Proposed legislation should never be presented as enacted law.

Human trafficking

Oklahoma criminal law addresses human trafficking and child trafficking. Legal definitions and penalties are fact- and statute-specific.

85% service rules

Oklahoma has statutes identifying certain offenses subject to enhanced minimum service requirements before parole consideration. UNgroomed should identify the exact statute and offense rather than applying an 85% rule universally.

For Abusers

STOP. If you are thinking about crossing a sexual or personal boundary with a child or another person who cannot freely consent, stop now. Do not rationalize it, test the boundary, create secrecy, seek access, or tell yourself that you will “go further later.” Distance yourself from the situation and get qualified help.

The realization

Your urges, excuses, history, loneliness, anger, entitlement, or attraction do not give you permission to harm another person. A person you target is not an opportunity, a fantasy, a secret, a possession, or a problem to manage. They are a human being whose safety and autonomy matter.

Grooming is not harmless flirting. Manipulation is not consent. Silence is not permission. Fear is not agreement. A child's trust is not an invitation.

A strong plea: never offend — never reoffend.

If you have not offended: do not start. Leave the situation, remove access, tell a qualified professional what is happening, and build a life that does not depend on another person's vulnerability.

If you have offended: do not do it again. Follow every court order and supervision requirement, complete required treatment, avoid prohibited situations, and seek specialized professional help. Taking responsibility means changing behavior, not finding better excuses.

Criminal penalties are real

Oklahoma criminal law contains multiple offenses involving child abuse, sexual abuse, grooming, exploitation and trafficking. Penalties depend on the exact offense and facts. Oklahoma's current statutes can authorize felony punishment, including substantial incarceration and fines; some repeat-offense provisions can carry especially severe consequences.

Read current Oklahoma Title 21.

Registration & supervision

Certain convictions can trigger sex-offender registration and other restrictions. Requirements depend on the offense, conviction and applicable law. Do not assume that a particular case has the same consequences as another.

Learn about federal SORNA resources.

Accountability can begin today

Prevention is not a single promise. It is a pattern of choices: no access to potential victims, no boundary testing, no secret relationships, no sexualized contact with minors, honest disclosure to qualified professionals, and consistent treatment and accountability.

Research summarized by the U.S. Department of Justice indicates that some specialized treatment approaches can reduce sexual recidivism for some offenders. Treatment is not an excuse or a guarantee; it is one part of responsible risk management and prevention.

Read the DOJ research summary.

If someone may be in immediate danger: create distance from the potential victim and contact emergency services or an appropriate child-protection/law-enforcement authority. Do not investigate the situation yourself or pressure anyone into secrecy.
